Vol. XIX — The Passage

On a foggy eve in November 1890, the gas lamps of Baker Street flickered as the veil between worlds grew thin. A card had been left on the mantel of the Midnight Gazette. It was plain until it was touched.

What followed was not a trick of the hand. The card remembered a street the fog had swallowed, and the street remembered a book. These cabinets are that book, taken down and set out. Open a plate to read the leaf. When you are finished, the parlor is still there.

Cabinet I · Leaf 3

The Fog of Westminster

The plate shows the clock tower from the bridge, which is as much of Westminster as the fog allowed that week. A map in the Gazette office draws the neighboring streets only while you are not looking at them. Westminster keeps its outline. The streets around it step aside.

Baker Street Gaslight

On Baker Street the lamps answer one another. When the third lamp from the corner goes dark, the parlor door is already open, whether or not anyone lifted the latch.

Cabinet II · Leaf 1

Shadow Manuscripts

The Gazette keeps a book it does not catalog. The pages in the plate are pinned so they cannot turn themselves, which they have tried. The names inside are written in a hand that changes with the weather. On wet nights the ink rises, as if the street were trying to remember who walked it.

The Alchemist's Seal

The seal is wax the color of old blood. Pressed into it is a circle that will not sit still if you look away. Collectors have tried to copy it. The copies dry blank.

Cabinet III · Leaf 7

Chronicles of Old Thames

This plate was made long after the night it describes. The river is the same river. The tide tables in the cabinet are annotated in a hand no one at the Gazette admits to, and the notes disagree with the Admiralty by exactly the time it takes to cross one more bridge than London has.

Marginal note

Tower Bridge was still a rumor of iron in the autumn of 1890. The chronicle records it anyway, which the editors have chosen not to correct.

Cabinet IV · Leaf 8

Phantasmagoria & Mirrors

The plate shows a house doubled in still water. Hold it beside the parlor mirror and the two reflections do not agree. Ours answers a minute late. Guests who check their collars in it are already in the hall, putting on a coat they have not yet reached for.

Obsidian Oracles

Seven stones, each answering a question you have not asked. They refuse any question you have. The editors consider this an improvement on most correspondence.

Cabinet V · Leaf 12

Beyond the Iron Gates

The road in the plate ends at a gate, which is as far as the surveyor would go. Past it, the exhibition does not end. It only pretends to. Visitors who step through report a second parlor, identical except that the card on the mantel is already face up.

Transmutation

Lead shot left in a saucer; by morning, a thin skin of gold. The saucer is exhibited empty. The gold is in the ledger, under expenses.

Cabinet VI · The leaf you turned

The Etheric Passage

There is no plate for this cabinet. You are the record of it. The page was warm when it landed, and the parlor agreed, for once, with the time in both rooms.

Spirits of Victorian Parlors

Calling cards left by guests who were never shown in. The astrolabe beside the tray is set for a sky London will not see until spring. Both are filed here so they will not follow you back to the mantel.
